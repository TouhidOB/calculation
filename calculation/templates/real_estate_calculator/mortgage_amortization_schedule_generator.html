What Is a Mortgage Amortization Schedule?

A mortgage amortization schedule is a complete table of every payment over your loan's term, showing how much goes to interest, how much to principal, and the balance remaining. It reveals the true cost of borrowing and how slowly equity builds in the early years. This detailed calculation is essential for understanding any home loan.

How Is the Schedule Calculated?

The calculator applies the amortization formula to find a fixed monthly payment, then for each month charges interest on the current balance and applies the rest to principal. As the balance falls, the interest portion shrinks and the principal portion grows. Repeating this calculation for every month produces the full schedule.

Why Does Early Interest Dominate?

Interest is charged on the outstanding balance, which is largest at the start of the loan. So early payments are mostly interest with little principal reduction. Over time, as the balance drops, more of each payment attacks the principal. Seeing this in the schedule explains why extra early payments save so much interest.

Frequently Asked Questions

How can I pay off my mortgage faster?

Make extra principal payments, especially early when interest dominates. Each extra dollar reduces the balance and saves future interest across the schedule.

What is the difference between principal and interest?

Principal is the amount you borrowed; interest is the lender's charge for the loan. Each payment covers interest first, then reduces principal.

Does a shorter term save money?

Yes. A shorter term means higher monthly payments but far less total interest, since the balance is paid down faster.
