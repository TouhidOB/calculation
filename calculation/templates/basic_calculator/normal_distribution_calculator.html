What Is a Normal Distribution?

A normal distribution is a symmetric, bell-shaped curve defined by its mean (μ) and standard deviation (σ). The mean sets the center; the standard deviation sets the spread. Most values cluster near the mean and taper off toward the tails. This shape models so many real-world variables that it's central to statistical calculation.

What Is a Z-Score?

A Z-score standardizes a value by measuring how many standard deviations it sits from the mean: Z = (X − μ) ÷ σ. For a mean of 100 and standard deviation of 15, a value of 130 has Z = (130−100)/15 = 2. A positive Z is above the mean, negative is below. The calculator performs this conversion instantly.

What Is Cumulative Probability?

Cumulative probability is the area under the normal curve to the left of a given X — the chance of observing a value at or below it. A Z-score of 0 (the mean) gives 0.5, since half the data lies below the center. The calculator computes this area, replacing the need to look up Z-tables for the probability calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the 68-95-99.7 rule?

In a normal distribution, about 68% of values fall within one standard deviation of the mean, 95% within two, and 99.7% within three. It's a quick way to gauge how typical a value is.

What does a negative Z-score mean?

The value is below the mean. A Z of −1.5 means the value sits 1.5 standard deviations beneath the average.

Where is the normal distribution used?

Standardized testing, quality control, finance risk models, and scientific measurement all rely on it to assess probabilities and identify outliers.
