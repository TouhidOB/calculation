What Is the Least Common Multiple?

The least common multiple (LCM) of two numbers is the smallest positive integer that is a multiple of both. For 12 and 18, the multiples of 12 are 12, 24, 36... and of 18 are 18, 36... — the first shared value is 36. The LCM is the foundation of adding fractions with different denominators and many scheduling calculations.

How Does the Calculator Find the LCM?

It uses the relationship LCM(a, b) = (a × b) ÷ GCF(a, b). For 12 and 18, the GCF is 6, so the LCM = (12 × 18) ÷ 6 = 216 ÷ 6 = 36. This method is far faster than listing multiples, since the GCF comes quickly from the Euclidean algorithm — making the calculation efficient for large numbers.

Where Is the LCM Used?

The LCM appears throughout math and daily life. Adding 1/12 + 1/18 requires the LCM 36 as the common denominator. It also solves cycle-overlap problems — if two buses depart every 12 and 18 minutes, they leave together every 36 minutes. Gear ratios and repeating schedules rely on the same calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the LCM of 12 and 18?

36, the smallest number both 12 and 18 divide into. The calculator returns it instantly using the GCF relationship.

What's the difference between LCM and GCF?

The LCM is the smallest shared multiple; the GCF is the largest shared factor. LCM is used for common denominators; GCF for simplifying. They satisfy LCM × GCF = a × b.

Can the LCM be one of the numbers?

Yes. If one number is a multiple of the other, the larger is the LCM. For example, LCM(4, 12) = 12, since 12 is already a multiple of 4.
