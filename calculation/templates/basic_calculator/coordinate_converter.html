Coordinate Converter: How Do You Convert Polar and Rectangular?

A point in a plane can be described two ways — by horizontal and vertical distances (x, y) or by distance and angle from the origin (r, θ). Switching between them requires trigonometry, and a misplaced sine or cosine wrecks the result. The Coordinate calculator converts both directions instantly, handling every trig calculation for you.

Key Takeaways
  • Rectangular (x, y) gives position; polar (r, θ) gives distance and angle.
  • Rectangular to polar: r = √(x² + y²) and θ = arctan(y/x).
  • Polar to rectangular: x = r·cos(θ) and y = r·sin(θ).
  • The calculator converts both ways with quadrant adjustments.

What Are Rectangular and Polar Coordinates?

Rectangular coordinates locate a point using perpendicular x and y distances; polar coordinates use a radius r and angle θ. Both describe the same point in different languages. A point three right and four up is (3, 4) rectangular — the same point is (5, 53.13°) polar. The converter translates fluently between these views.

How Do You Convert Rectangular to Polar?

Compute the radius as r = √(x² + y²) and the angle as θ = arctan(y/x). For (3, 4): r = √(9+16) = 5 and θ = arctan(4/3) ≈ 53.13°. The calculator also resolves the quadrant correctly, since arctan alone can't distinguish points in opposite quadrants — preventing an angle that's 180° off.

How Do You Convert Polar to Rectangular?

Use x = r·cos(θ) and y = r·sin(θ). For (5, 53.13°): x = 5·cos(53.13°) ≈ 3 and y = 5·sin(53.13°) ≈ 4, recovering the original (3, 4). The calculator evaluates the trig functions precisely, so you never need a function table.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the radius r in polar coordinates?

The straight-line distance from the origin to the point, always non-negative, computed as r = √(x² + y²) — the Pythagorean distance.

Why does the quadrant matter?

Basic arctan only returns angles between −90° and 90°, so it can't tell a first-quadrant point from a third-quadrant one. The calculator checks the signs of x and y to place θ correctly.

Are degrees or radians used?

Both are valid; degrees suit engineering and navigation, radians suit calculus and physics, where 180° = π radians.
