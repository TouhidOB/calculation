What Is the Greatest Common Factor?

The GCF, also called the greatest common divisor, is the largest whole number that divides two integers with no remainder. For 36 and 48, the common factors are 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12 — the largest is 12. Knowing this lets you simplify 36/48 straight to 3/4 in a single step, the foundation of fraction-reduction calculation.

How Does the Euclidean Algorithm Find the GCF?

It repeatedly replaces the larger number with the remainder of dividing it by the smaller, until the remainder is zero — the last nonzero remainder is the GCF. For GCF(48, 36): 48÷36 = 1 r12, then 36÷12 = 3 r0, so the GCF is 12. This method needs only a couple of divisions even for huge numbers, which is why the calculator uses it.

Why Is the Euclidean Algorithm Faster?

It shrinks the numbers rapidly with each division, instead of testing every possible factor. Listing factors of large numbers is laborious, but repeated division converges in just a few steps. For values with hundreds of digits, the Euclidean method is the only practical approach — and the calculator applies it instantly regardless of size.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the GCF of 36 and 48?

12, the largest number dividing both evenly. The calculator returns it instantly, ready for simplifying 36/48 to 3/4.

What's the difference between GCF and LCM?

The GCF is the largest shared factor; the LCM is the smallest shared multiple. GCF simplifies fractions; LCM finds common denominators. GCF × LCM equals the product of the numbers.

Can the GCF be 1?

Yes. When two numbers share no factor besides 1, their GCF is 1 and they're called coprime, like GCF(8, 15) = 1.
