What Is Euler's Number?

Euler's number, written e, is an irrational constant approximately 2.71828, defined as the base rate of continuous growth. One classic definition is the limit of (1 + 1/n)ⁿ as n grows without bound. As you compound more frequently, the result converges on e, which is why it governs continuous compound interest and decay calculation.

How Do You Calculate e^x?

Enter a decimal exponent and the calculator raises e to that power, evaluating the natural exponential function. e⁰ = 1, e¹ ≈ 2.71828, and e² ≈ 7.38906. As x increases, e^x grows rapidly — the signature of exponential growth that drives compound interest formulas like A = Pe^(rt).

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the value of e?

Approximately 2.718281828459045, an irrational and transcendental constant. It's the base of the natural logarithm, and its decimal expansion never ends or repeats.

Why is e called the natural base?

Because e^x is the only exponential function that equals its own derivative — its growth rate equals its current value at every point.

What does e^0 equal?

e^0 equals 1, like any nonzero number raised to the power zero. With zero exponent, no growth has occurred, so the multiplier is 1.
