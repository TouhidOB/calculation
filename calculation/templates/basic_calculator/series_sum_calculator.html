Calculate the sum of the first $\mathbf{n}$ terms for Arithmetic and Geometric Progressions.

Arithmetic Series ($S_n$)

Formula: $S_n = \frac{n}{2} [2a + (n-1)d]$

Geometric Series ($S_n$)

Formula: $S_n = a \frac{1 - r^n}{1 - r}$ (for $r \neq 1$)

  • An arithmetic series adds a constant difference each term.

What Is a Series?

A series is the sum of the terms of a sequence. An arithmetic sequence grows by adding a fixed common difference (2, 5, 8, 11...), while a geometric sequence grows by multiplying by a fixed ratio (3, 6, 12, 24...). Summing many terms by hand is tedious, but the right formula collapses the whole calculation into one step.

How Do You Sum an Arithmetic Series?

Use Sₙ = n/2 × (2a + (n−1)d), where a is the first term, d the common difference, and n the number of terms. For 2 + 5 + 8 + 11 + 14 (a=2, d=3, n=5): Sₙ = 5/2 × (4 + 12) = 2.5 × 16 = 40. The calculator applies this formula instantly, no matter how many terms.

How Do You Sum a Geometric Series?

Use Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ) ÷ (1 − r), where r is the common ratio. For 3 + 6 + 12 + 24 (a=3, r=2, n=4): Sₙ = 3(1 − 16) ÷ (1 − 2) = 3(−15) ÷ (−1) = 45. Geometric sums grow fast because each term multiplies, and the calculator handles the exponent calculation precisely.

Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between arithmetic and geometric?

Arithmetic series add a constant difference between terms; geometric series multiply by a constant ratio. The growth pattern and sum formula differ accordingly.

Can a geometric series have a ratio less than 1?

Yes. A ratio between 0 and 1 makes terms shrink. The same formula applies, and such series approach a finite limit as terms increase.

Where are series used?

Finance (loan payments, annuities), physics, and computer science all use series sums to total repeating quantities efficiently.
