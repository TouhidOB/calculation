When you plot paired data, the points rarely fall on a perfect line — but a line of best fit reveals the underlying trend. Key Takeaways
  • Linear regression finds the straight line that best fits paired data.
  • The least squares method minimizes the total squared distance from points to the line.
  • The result is the equation ŷ = mx + b (slope and y-intercept).

What Is Linear Regression?

Linear regression models the relationship between two variables by fitting a straight line through their data points. The independent variable X predicts the dependent variable Y. The resulting line, ŷ = mx + b, lets you estimate Y for any X. It's one of the most widely used tools in statistics, science, and business for trend calculation and forecasting.

How Does the Least Squares Method Work?

The least squares method finds the line that minimizes the sum of the squared vertical distances between each data point and the line. Squaring ensures positive and negative gaps don't cancel and penalizes large misses more. The calculator applies the standard formulas for slope and intercept across all your points, producing the single best-fit line in one calculation.

What Do the Slope and Intercept Mean?

The slope m shows how much Y changes for each unit increase in X — the strength and direction of the relationship. The y-intercept b is the predicted Y when X is zero. Together they define the trend line. A positive slope means Y rises with X; a negative slope means it falls.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the line of best fit?

The straight line that comes closest to all data points, minimizing total squared error. It captures the overall trend even when individual points scatter around it.

How many data points do I need?

At least two, but more points give a more reliable trend. Each X value should have a matching Y value, so both lists must be the same length for the calculation.

What is regression used for?

Forecasting sales, analyzing scientific experiments, modeling cost trends, and any task where you predict one variable from another using historical data.
