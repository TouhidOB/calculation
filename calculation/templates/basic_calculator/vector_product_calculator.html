Vector Product Calculator: How Do You Find the Dot and Cross Product?

Two 3D vectors can be multiplied two fundamentally different ways: the dot product yields a number, the cross product yields a new vector. Both are central to physics and 3D graphics, but their formulas are easy to confuse. The Vector Product calculator computes the dot product, cross product, and magnitudes for vectors A and B in one calculation. Enter both vectors' components.

Key Takeaways
  • The dot product returns a scalar (number); the cross product returns a vector.
  • Dot product: A·B = AₓBₓ + AᵧBᵧ + A_zB_z.
  • The cross product is perpendicular to both input vectors.
  • A zero dot product means the vectors are perpendicular.

What Is the Dot Product?

The dot product multiplies two vectors to produce a single number, computed as A·B = AₓBₓ + AᵧBᵧ + A_zB_z. It measures how much the vectors point in the same direction. A positive result means a similar direction, zero means perpendicular, and negative means opposing. The dot product is key to projection and angle calculation between vectors.

What Is the Cross Product?

The cross product multiplies two 3D vectors to produce a third vector perpendicular to both. Its magnitude equals the area of the parallelogram the vectors span, and its direction follows the right-hand rule. The cross product is essential for finding normals, torque, and rotational axes — a calculation the tool handles component by component.

When Are Vectors Perpendicular or Parallel?

Two vectors are perpendicular when their dot product is zero, and parallel when their cross product is the zero vector. These conditions reveal geometric relationships instantly. Frequently Asked Questions

What's the difference between dot and cross product?

The dot product gives a scalar measuring alignment; the cross product gives a vector perpendicular to both inputs. They answer different geometric questions.

What does a zero dot product mean?

The two vectors are perpendicular (at 90°). Their directions share no common component.

Where are vector products used?

Physics (work, torque), 3D graphics (surface normals, lighting), and engineering all rely on dot and cross products for direction and force calculation.
