What Is a Factor?

A factor of a number is any whole number that divides it exactly, leaving no remainder. For example, 3 is a factor of 12 because 12 ÷ 3 = 4. Every positive integer has at least two factors: 1 and itself. Numbers with exactly two factors are prime; numbers with more are composite. Identifying factors is fundamental to nearly every number-theory calculation.

How Do You Find All the Factors?

Test each integer to see which divide evenly — but a smarter method checks only up to the square root, since factors pair up. For 12: 1×12, 2×6, 3×4 give factors 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12. The calculator uses this efficient approach, so the calculation stays fast even for large numbers.

Why Do Factors Come in Pairs?

Factors come in pairs because division is reciprocal: if a divides n, then n/a also divides n. Each factor below the square root has a matching partner above it. Perfect squares like 100 have one factor (10) that pairs with itself, giving an odd total count — a neat signal that a number is a perfect square.

Frequently Asked Questions

What are the factors of 12?

1, 2, 3, 4, 6, and 12 — the six whole numbers that divide 12 with no remainder, forming three pairs: (1,12), (2,6), (3,4).

What's the difference between a factor and a multiple?

A factor divides a number evenly; a multiple is the result of multiplying it. So 3 is a factor of 12, and 12 is a multiple of 3.

How many factors does a prime have?

Exactly two: 1 and itself. If the calculator returns just two factors, the number you entered is prime.
