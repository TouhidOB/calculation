What Is a Factorial?

A factorial, written n!, is the product of every positive integer from 1 up to n. It counts the number of ways to arrange n distinct objects in order, making it the foundation of permutations, combinations, and probability calculation. For instance, 4! = 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 24, meaning there are 24 ways to arrange four items.

How Do You Calculate 7 Factorial?

Multiply all integers from 7 down to 1: 7×6=42, ×5=210, ×4=840, ×3=2,520, ×2=5,040, ×1=5,040. So 7! = 5,040. Each step builds on the last, and the product grows rapidly — a calculation the tool evaluates instantly even for much larger inputs.

Why Does the Factorial Grow So Fast?

Factorials grow faster than exponential functions because each step multiplies by an ever-larger number. By 20!, the result exceeds a standard 64-bit integer's range (20! ≈ 2.4 × 10¹⁸). The calculator sidesteps this limit with arbitrary-precision BigInt arithmetic, keeping every digit exact.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a factorial used for?

Factorials count arrangements and underpin permutations and combinations in probability and statistics. They appear in lottery odds, Taylor series, and binomial coefficients.

Why is 0! equal to 1?

Because there's exactly one way to arrange zero objects — the empty arrangement. It also keeps formulas like nCr = n!/(r!(n−r)!) valid.

Can you take the factorial of a decimal?

The standard factorial is defined only for non-negative integers. Factorials of non-integers require the gamma function, an advanced extension.
