What Is a Complex Number?

A complex number combines a real and an imaginary part in the form a+bi, where i = √(−1). In 3 + 4i, the real part is 3 and the imaginary part is 4. Complex numbers solve equations like x² + 1 = 0 that have no real solutions, and they're indispensable wherever waves, oscillation, or rotation appear in calculation.

How Do You Add and Subtract Complex Numbers?

Combine the real parts together and the imaginary parts together separately: (a₁+b₁i) ± (a₂+b₂i) = (a₁±a₂) + (b₁±b₂)i. Adding (3 + 4i) and (1 + 2i) gives real 3+1=4 and imaginary 4+2=6, so the result is 4 + 6i. The calculator keeps the components cleanly separated.

How Does Complex Multiplication Work?

Multiplication uses the distributive (FOIL) method, then applies i² = −1. Multiplying (3 + 4i)(1 + 2i): 3 + 6i + 4i + 8i² = 3 + 10i − 8 = −5 + 10i. This sign flip from i² is where manual calculation most often goes wrong — the calculator handles it every time.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the imaginary unit i?

i is defined as the square root of −1, so i² = −1. It lets numbers like √(−9) = 3i exist and is the foundation of all complex number calculation.

Why use the conjugate for division?

The conjugate a−bi turns the denominator into a real number, since (a+bi)(a−bi) = a²+b². The calculator applies it automatically to produce a clean a+bi result.

What are complex numbers used for?

AC circuit analysis, signal processing, control systems, and quantum mechanics — any phenomenon involving waves or rotation benefits from complex calculation.
