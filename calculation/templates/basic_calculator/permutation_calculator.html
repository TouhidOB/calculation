Key Takeaways
  • A permutation counts arrangements where order DOES matter.

What Is a Permutation?

A permutation is an arrangement of items where the order matters. Choosing a president, vice-president, and treasurer from a group is a permutation, because swapping roles creates a different outcome. The notation nPr, read "n permute r," counts how many ordered arrangements of r items you can make from n distinct items — a core combinatorics calculation.

What Is the Permutation Formula?

The formula is nPr = n! ÷ (n − r)!. The (n − r)! in the denominator removes the arrangements of the items you didn't choose. For 10 people in 3 ordered positions: 10P3 = 10! ÷ 7! = 10 × 9 × 8 = 720. The calculator runs this factorial calculation instantly, even for large n.

How Do Permutations Differ From Combinations?

Permutations count order; combinations don't. That's why nPr is always larger than nCr — each unordered combination corresponds to r! different ordered permutations. Picking 3 from 10 gives 720 permutations but only 120 combinations. Use a permutation when rearranging the chosen items produces a genuinely different result.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does nPr mean?

"n permute r" — the number of ordered arrangements of r items chosen from n. For example, 5P2 = 20, meaning 20 ordered ways to pick two from five.

When should I use a permutation instead of a combination?

Use a permutation when order matters, like rankings, race finishes, or passwords. Use a combination when order is irrelevant, like committee membership.

What is nPn?

It equals n!, the number of ways to arrange all n items in order. For example, 4P4 = 4! = 24.
