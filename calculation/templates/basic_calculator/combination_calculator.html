Combination Calculator ($\text{nCr}$)

Calculates the number of ways to select $r$ items from $n$ items, where **order does not matter**.

The Combination Formula:

$$\mathbf{\text{C}(n, r) = \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r! (n-r)!}}$$

What is a Combination Calculator?

A Combination Calculator determines the number of possible ways to select a specific number of items from a larger group, where the order of selection typically does not matter. This is mathematically represented as nCr.

Combinations vs. Permutations: What's the Difference?

The key difference is Order.

  • Permutation (nPr): Order matters. (e.g., A password "123" is different from "321").
  • Combination (nCr): Order does NOT matter. (e.g., A fruit salad with "Apple, Banana" is the same as "Banana, Apple").

Real-Life Examples of Combinations

🎰 Lottery Odds

In a lottery where you pick 6 numbers out of 49, the order you pick them doesn't change your winning status. This is a classic combination problem.

🃏 Poker Hands

When you are dealt 5 cards, it doesn't matter which order they arrived in. A "Royal Flush" is the same hand regardless of card deal order.

👥 Team Selection

Choosing 3 students from a class of 20 to represent the school is a combination, provided positions (like President vs. VP) aren't assigned.

The Math Behind It

The formula for combinations is:

$$\mathbf{C(n, r) = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}}$$

Where:

  • n = Total number of items in the set.
  • r = Number of items you are choosing.
  • ! = Factorial (e.g., 5! = 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1).

What Is a Combination?

A combination is a selection from a larger group where order doesn't matter. Picking apples, bananas, and cherries is the same combination no matter the sequence — unlike a permutation, where order counts. The notation is C(n, r) or nCr: from n distinct items, how many groups of size r can you form?

What Is the Combination Formula?

The formula is nCr = n! / (r! · (n−r)!). Dividing by r! removes duplicate orderings that a permutation would count, giving only unique selections. For C(10, 3): 10! / (3! · 7!) = 3,628,800 / (6 · 5,040) = 120. So there are 120 distinct three-person committees from ten people — a calculation the tool runs instantly.

Combinations vs. Permutations: What's the Difference?

The key difference is order: combinations ignore it, permutations count it. That's why permutations are always equal or larger — every combination maps to multiple ordered arrangements (nPr = nCr · r!). Use a combination when rearranging the chosen items creates no genuinely different outcome.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does nCr mean?

"n choose r" — the number of ways to select r items from n when order doesn't matter. For example, 5C2 equals 10. The calculator computes this value directly.

Can r be larger than n?

No. You can't choose more items than exist, so r must be ≤ n. The calculator rejects inputs where r exceeds n.

What is nC0 and nCn?

Both equal 1. There's exactly one way to choose nothing and one way to choose everything — which is why the first and last numbers in every Pascal's triangle row are 1.
