What Does Simplifying a Fraction Mean?

It means rewriting a fraction with the smallest possible whole-number numerator and denominator while keeping the same value. The fractions 12/18, 6/9, and 2/3 are all equal, but 2/3 is the simplest — the goal of every reduction calculation. A fraction reaches lowest terms when the top and bottom share no common factor other than 1.

How Do You Reduce a Fraction Using the GCF?

Find the greatest common factor of numerator and denominator, then divide both by it. For 12/18: the GCF is 6, so 12÷6 = 2 and 18÷6 = 3, giving 2/3. The GCF is the largest number that divides both evenly, so dividing by it collapses the fraction to lowest terms in one clean calculation.

Why Use the Greatest Common Factor?

Using the GCF guarantees the fraction is fully reduced in a single division. Dividing 12/18 by 2 first only gives 6/9, still needing another step. The GCF approach reaches 2/3 directly — which is exactly how the calculator works, finding the GCF and reducing in one pass.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you simplify 12/18?

Divide both by their GCF of 6: 12÷6 = 2 and 18÷6 = 3, giving 2/3. The calculator does this automatically without manual factoring.

Can every fraction be simplified?

No. A fraction is already in lowest terms if its numerator and denominator are coprime, like 3/5. The calculator returns such fractions unchanged.

Does simplifying change the value?

No. 12/18 and 2/3 are exactly equal. Dividing numerator and denominator by the same number preserves the ratio throughout the calculation.
