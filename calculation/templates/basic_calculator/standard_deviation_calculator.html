What Is Standard Deviation?

Standard deviation quantifies the typical distance of data points from their mean. Two data sets can share the same average yet differ wildly in consistency — standard deviation captures that difference. A low σ signals reliable, tightly grouped values; a high σ signals volatility. It's one of the most important measures in statistical calculation.

How Is Standard Deviation Calculated?

The process has four steps: find the mean, subtract it from each value and square the result, average those squared deviations (that's the variance), then take the square root. The square root returns the measure to the original units. The calculator performs this entire calculation across your data set instantly.

What Is the Difference Between Variance and Standard Deviation?

Variance is the average squared deviation from the mean; standard deviation is its square root. Variance is in squared units, which can be hard to interpret, while standard deviation is in the same units as the data — making σ more intuitive. The calculator reports both so you can use whichever your analysis needs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does a high standard deviation mean?

Data points are spread far from the mean — high variability. In finance, this signals risk; in manufacturing, inconsistency.

What's the difference between population and sample σ?

Population standard deviation divides by N; sample divides by N−1. This calculator computes the population standard deviation for a complete data set.

Why square the deviations?

Squaring makes all deviations positive so they don't cancel, and it penalizes larger gaps more heavily, giving a meaningful measure of spread.
