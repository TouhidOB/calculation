Percentage Change Calculator: How Do You Find Percent Increase or Decrease?

Whether a stock rose, a price dropped, or a population grew, percentage change tells you the size of the move relative to where it started. Computing it by hand means subtracting, dividing by the original, and converting — easy to invert. The Percentage Change calculator finds the increase or decrease between two values in one calculation. Enter the initial and final values to get the percent change.

Key Takeaways
  • Percentage change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100.
  • A positive result is an increase; a negative result is a decrease.
  • From 100 to 125 is a 25% increase; from 100 to 75 is a 25% decrease.
  • The change is always measured relative to the initial value.

What Is Percentage Change?

Percentage change expresses the difference between two values as a percentage of the starting value. It standardizes comparisons: a $10 rise means more on a $20 item than on a $200 one. The formula is (V_final − V_initial) ÷ V_initial × 100, and this single calculation works for both increases and decreases.

How Do You Calculate Percent Increase?

Subtract the initial value from the final, divide by the initial, then multiply by 100. Going from 100 to 125: (125 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = 25%. The calculator handles each step, so you never divide by the wrong number — the denominator is always the original value, the key to a correct calculation.

How Is Percent Decrease Different?

The formula is identical; the result is simply negative. From 100 to 75: (75 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = −25%, a 25% decrease. The sign tells the direction. Note that a 25% increase followed by a 25% decrease does not return to the start, because each percentage applies to a different base.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the percentage change from 100 to 125?

A 25% increase. Subtract 100 from 125 to get 25, divide by 100, and multiply by 100. The calculator returns this instantly.

Why divide by the initial value?

Because percentage change measures the move relative to the starting point. Dividing by the final value instead would answer a different question and give a wrong result.

Can percentage change exceed 100%?

Yes. If a value more than doubles, the increase is over 100%. Going from 50 to 150 is a 200% increase.
