What Is the Modulo Operation?

Modulo, written A mod N, gives the remainder after dividing A (the dividend) by N (the divisor). For 17 mod 5: 17 divided by 5 is 3 with remainder 2, so the result is 2. The quotient is discarded; only the leftover matters. This single calculation drives any logic that cycles or repeats at fixed intervals.

How Does the Calculator Compute A mod N?

It divides A by N, keeps the whole-number quotient, and returns what's left over. Enter the dividend and divisor, and the calculator returns the remainder. For example, 23 mod 7 = 2, because 23 = 3×7 + 2. The result is always smaller than the divisor N, since any full multiple of N is removed.

Why Is Modulo Used in Programming?

Modulo is everywhere in code. It wraps values around a range (clock hours use mod 12), checks even or odd (n mod 2), distributes items into buckets for hashing, and triggers "every Nth" events. Cryptography relies heavily on modular arithmetic. The calculator mirrors exactly the remainder calculation a computer performs.
