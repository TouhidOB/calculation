What Is Pi?

Pi is the constant ratio between any circle's circumference and its diameter, approximately 3.14159. No matter the circle's size, dividing its circumference by its diameter always yields pi. It's irrational, meaning it can't be written as an exact fraction, and its decimal expansion is infinite and non-repeating — making every digit a fixed value worth a precise calculation.

Why Does Pi Matter So Much?

Pi appears far beyond circles — in trigonometry, calculus, physics, probability, and signal processing. Any calculation involving circles, waves, or oscillation invokes pi. Its digits are also used to test computing performance and random-number quality, and pi has become a cultural icon celebrated each March 14 (Pi Day).
