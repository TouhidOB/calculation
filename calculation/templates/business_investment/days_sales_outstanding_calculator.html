What Is Days Sales Outstanding?

Days Sales Outstanding is the average number of days a company takes to collect payment after making a credit sale. It measures how efficiently a business converts receivables into cash. A low DSO means customers pay quickly; a high DSO ties up working capital. This calculation is a key indicator of cash-flow health.

How Is DSO Calculated?

Divide accounts receivable by total credit sales, then multiply by the number of days in the period. If receivables are $50,000, credit sales are $300,000, and the period is 90 days, DSO = (50,000 ÷ 300,000) × 90 = 15 days. The calculator performs this calculation instantly.

Why Does DSO Matter for Cash Flow?

Every day money sits in unpaid invoices is a day you can't use it to pay suppliers, fund payroll, or invest. A rising DSO warns that collections are slowing, possibly due to lax credit terms or struggling customers. Monitoring this calculation helps you protect liquidity and act before cash flow tightens.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good DSO?

It depends on your payment terms, but a DSO close to or below your stated terms (e.g., net 30) indicates efficient collections.

How can I lower DSO?

Invoice promptly, offer early-payment discounts, tighten credit terms, and follow up on overdue accounts consistently.

Does DSO use cash sales?

No. DSO is based on credit sales, since cash sales are collected immediately and don't create receivables.
