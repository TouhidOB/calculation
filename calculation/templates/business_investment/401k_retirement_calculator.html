What Is a 401(k)?

A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement account that lets you invest pre-tax income, which grows tax-deferred until withdrawal. Many employers match a portion of your contributions, effectively adding free money. Projecting its growth requires a future-value calculation that compounds your balance and ongoing deposits over your working years.

How Does the Calculator Project Your Balance?

It compounds three streams: the future value of your current balance, the growth of all future employee contributions, and the employer match on those contributions. Each is grown at your expected annual return over the years until retirement. The calculator sums them into one projected total, turning a complex multi-part calculation into a single figure.

Why Does the Employer Match Matter So Much?

An employer match is an immediate, guaranteed return on your contribution — often 50% or 100% up to a limit. Failing to contribute enough to earn the full match leaves free money on the table. Over decades, that matched money compounds alongside your own, dramatically lifting the final calculation.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much should I contribute to my 401(k)?

At minimum, enough to capture the full employer match. Many advisors suggest 10-15% of income, but the calculator lets you test different rates to see the impact on your final balance.

What return rate should I assume?

Historical stock-market returns average around 7-10% annually before inflation. Using a conservative estimate produces a more reliable retirement calculation.

Why does starting early matter?

Compound growth rewards time. A dollar invested at 25 has decades to multiply, while the same dollar at 50 has far fewer compounding years.
