What Is Dividend Yield?

Dividend yield is the annual dividend a stock pays expressed as a percentage of its current share price. It lets income-focused investors compare how much cash different stocks return per dollar invested. A higher yield means more income relative to price, making this calculation central to dividend and value investing strategies.

How Is Dividend Yield Calculated?

Divide the annual dividend per share by the current share price, then multiply by 100. If a stock pays $2 per share annually and trades at $50, the yield is (2 ÷ 50) × 100 = 4%. The calculator performs this calculation instantly, letting you compare income across stocks.

Why Does Yield Move With Price?

Because price is the denominator, yield rises when the share price falls and drops when it climbs, even if the dividend stays the same. A very high yield can therefore signal a falling stock price rather than generous payouts. Always check why the yield is high — this calculation tells only part of the story.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good dividend yield?

Often 2-5% is considered solid for established companies. Unusually high yields may indicate risk or a declining share price, so investigate further.

Can a high yield be a warning sign?

Yes. A spiking yield often reflects a falling price, which may signal trouble. A sustainable, growing dividend matters more than a high headline yield.

Does yield include stock price gains?

No. Yield measures only dividend income. Total return adds price appreciation to dividends for the complete picture.
