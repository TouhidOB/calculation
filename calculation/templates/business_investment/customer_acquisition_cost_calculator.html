What Is Customer Acquisition Cost?

Customer Acquisition Cost is the total expense of acquiring a new customer, including all sales and marketing spending over a period divided by the number of customers gained. It tells you whether your growth engine is efficient or burning cash. Tracking this calculation is essential for sustainable, profitable scaling.

How Is CAC Calculated?

Add up all sales and marketing costs for a period, then divide by the number of new customers acquired in that same period. If you spent $50,000 and gained 500 customers, your CAC is 50,000 ÷ 500 = $100 per customer. The calculator performs this calculation instantly, giving a clear cost-per-customer figure.

Why Compare CAC to Lifetime Value?

CAC only makes sense alongside customer lifetime value (CLV). If a customer costs $100 to acquire but generates $400 over their relationship, the economics work. Many investors look for a CLV:CAC ratio of at least 3:1. Pairing this calculation with CLV reveals whether your growth is truly profitable.

Frequently Asked Questions

What costs go into CAC?

All sales and marketing expenses: ad spend, salaries, software, content, and commissions tied to acquiring customers in the period.

What is a good CAC?

There's no universal number — it depends on your CLV. CAC is healthy when it's comfortably below the revenue a customer generates over time.

How can I lower CAC?

Improve targeting, boost conversion rates, leverage referrals and organic channels, and retain customers so word-of-mouth lowers paid spend.
