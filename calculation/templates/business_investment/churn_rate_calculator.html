What Is Churn Rate?

Churn rate measures the proportion of customers who stop doing business with you during a specific period, usually a month or year. For subscription and SaaS companies, it's a vital health metric — high churn means you must constantly replace lost customers just to stay flat. Tracking this calculation reveals how well your product retains users.

How Is Churn Rate Calculated?

Subtract your end-of-period customer count from the start, divide by the starting count, and multiply by 100. If you began with 500 customers and ended with 460, you lost 40, so churn = (40 ÷ 500) × 100 = 8%. The calculator performs this calculation instantly, giving a clear retention snapshot.

Why Does Churn Matter So Much?

Churn compounds. A 5% monthly churn loses nearly half your customers over a year. It also raises the pressure on customer acquisition, since you must replace departed customers before growing. Reducing churn even slightly dramatically improves lifetime value and revenue stability, making this calculation central to subscription strategy.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good churn rate?

It varies by industry, but lower is always better. Many SaaS firms target annual churn below 5-7%; monthly churn under 1-2% is considered strong.

What's the difference between customer and revenue churn?

Customer churn counts lost accounts; revenue churn measures lost recurring revenue. A business can lose small customers yet keep revenue stable.

How can I reduce churn?

Improve onboarding, deliver ongoing value, address complaints quickly, and engage at-risk customers before they leave.
