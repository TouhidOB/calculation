What Is Asset Allocation?

Asset allocation is the way you divide your portfolio among categories like stocks, bonds, and cash. It's the single biggest driver of long-term risk and return. A target allocation reflects your goals and risk tolerance, and keeping to it requires periodic rebalancing — a calculation comparing current holdings against your desired percentages.

Why Does a Portfolio Drift?

Different assets grow at different rates. If stocks surge while bonds lag, your stock percentage rises above target, raising your overall risk. This drift is natural but unintended. The calculator measures each asset's current share against its target, revealing exactly how far the portfolio has wandered in one calculation.

How Does Rebalancing Work?

Rebalancing brings each asset back to its target by selling those that are overweight and buying those that are underweight. The calculator computes the precise dollar amount to trade for every holding, so you don't have to estimate. Following these moves restores your intended risk profile and locks in disciplined, unemotional investing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should I rebalance?

Common approaches are annually or when an asset drifts more than 5% from its target. The calculator helps you check your allocation any time.

Why is asset allocation important?

It controls your portfolio's risk and expected return more than individual stock picks. A disciplined allocation smooths volatility over time.

Does rebalancing guarantee higher returns?

No, but it manages risk and enforces buying low and selling high. It keeps your portfolio aligned with your long-term plan.
