Dollar-Cost Averaging Calculator: Does Investing Steadily Beat Timing the Market?

Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount at regular intervals regardless of price — buying more shares when cheap and fewer when expensive. The DCA calculator simulates this strategy across price fluctuations in one calculation. Enter your investment amount, frequency, and prices to see your average cost, total shares, and final value.

Key Takeaways
  • DCA invests a fixed sum at set intervals regardless of price.
  • It buys more shares when prices are low, fewer when high.
  • This lowers your average cost per share over volatile periods.
  • DCA removes the temptation to time the market.

What Is Dollar-Cost Averaging?

Dollar-cost averaging is an investing strategy where you put the same amount of money into an asset at regular intervals, no matter the price. Over time, this naturally buys more shares when prices dip and fewer when they spike, smoothing out volatility. The DCA calculation reveals how this disciplined approach plays out across a fluctuating market.

How Does the Calculator Simulate DCA?

It applies your fixed investment at each interval against the prices you provide, computing how many shares each contribution buys. Summing the shares and dividing total invested by total shares gives your average cost per share. The calculator then values your holdings at the final price, completing the calculation and showing your gain or loss.

Why Does DCA Lower Your Average Cost?

Because a fixed dollar amount buys more shares when prices are low, your purchases are weighted toward cheaper prices. This pulls your average cost below the simple average of the prices — a mathematical benefit of the strategy. The calculator makes this effect visible, helping you see why steady investing often beats nervous market timing.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does DCA guarantee profit?

No. It reduces the risk of investing everything at a peak, but returns still depend on the asset's long-term direction. DCA manages risk, not certainty.

Is DCA better than lump-sum investing?

Lump-sum often wins in steadily rising markets, but DCA reduces regret and volatility risk. It's especially suited to investing from regular income.

How often should I invest with DCA?

Common intervals are weekly or monthly. The key is consistency, which the calculator models across your chosen schedule.
