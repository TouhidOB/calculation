What Is the Current Ratio?

The current ratio is a liquidity metric comparing what a company owns that can be converted to cash within a year (current assets) against what it owes in the same period (current liabilities). It answers a critical question: can the business pay its short-term bills? This simple calculation is a cornerstone of financial analysis.

How Is the Current Ratio Calculated?

Divide current assets by current liabilities. If a company has $200,000 in current assets and $100,000 in current liabilities, the current ratio is 200,000 ÷ 100,000 = 2.0. This means it has twice the assets needed to cover its short-term debts. The calculator performs this calculation instantly.

What Does the Result Tell You?

A ratio above 1 means the company can cover its short-term obligations; the higher, the safer. But a very high ratio (above 3) may signal idle cash or inefficient asset use. A ratio below 1 warns that liabilities exceed liquid assets, raising solvency concerns. The calculator gives the figure you need to interpret this balance.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a good current ratio?

Generally between 1.5 and 3, indicating healthy liquidity without excessive idle assets. The ideal varies by industry.

How is it different from the quick ratio?

The quick ratio excludes inventory, offering a stricter liquidity test. The current ratio includes all current assets.

Can a high current ratio be bad?

Yes. An excessively high ratio may mean the company hoards cash or inventory instead of investing it productively.
